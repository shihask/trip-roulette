# Trip Roulette Kerala --- Product Requirements Document

**Document version:** 1.2 (security wording aligned with Architecture 1.2)\
**Date:** 4 November 2026\
**Product:** Trip Roulette Kerala\
**Platform:** Responsive website + Progressive Web App (PWA)\
**Initial market:** Kerala, India\
**Status:** Initial product baseline --- validate assumptions before
implementation

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Product summary

Trip Roulette Kerala helps people decide where to travel and turn that
decision into a practical trip plan.

A traveller enters a starting location, date or flexible travel window,
duration, budget per person, group size, travel party, interests and
transport mode. The system filters a curated destination catalogue,
ranks eligible destinations and presents exactly three meaningfully
different recommendations. The user spins a roulette wheel to select one
of those three. The platform then provides destination information,
transparent cost estimates and a suggested itinerary.

**Core promise:** "Don't know where to go? Let Kerala surprise you."

The roulette is the engaging interaction. Trustworthy recommendations
and useful planning information are the product's real value.

## 2. Problem and opportunity

Travellers often: - struggle to choose a destination; - discover places
through fragmented sources; - find it difficult to judge whether a trip
fits their time and budget; - plan group trips through lengthy
discussions; - encounter outdated information about access, fees, routes
and opening conditions.

Trip Roulette brings discovery and first-stage planning into one
experience. It must not imply that every destination is open, safe or
affordable unless the relevant information has been verified.

## 3. Goals

1.  Help a user move from "Where should we go?" to a suitable
    destination choice quickly.
2.  Return three eligible, diverse recommendations based on explicit
    preferences.
3.  Make the roulette selection fair and transparent.
4.  Show understandable estimates with assumptions and update dates.
5.  Provide a useful destination overview and itinerary.
6.  Support guest use, sharing and PWA installation.
7.  Build a curated, maintainable Kerala destination dataset.

### Proposed validation targets

These are hypotheses for the beta, not guaranteed industry benchmarks: -
Preference completion rate: 70% of users who start the wizard. -
Recommendation-to-spin rate: 60%. - Spin-to-destination-detail rate:
50%. - Trip share rate: 15%. - 30-day returning-user rate: 20%.

Review these targets after collecting a meaningful sample. Do not
optimize for clicks at the expense of recommendation quality.

## 4. Non-goals for MVP

The first release will not: - sell or book flights, trains, hotels or
tickets; - process payments; - provide live GPS tracking; - provide a
social feed or direct messaging; - support complex collaborative group
rooms or voting; - accept unmoderated public destination publishing; -
guarantee exact costs, travel times, weather or access conditions; -
replace official park, forest, tourism or transport sources.

## 6. Product principles

1.  **Constraints before AI:** hard eligibility rules are applied before
    ranking or AI-generated copy.
2.  **Data before claims:** destination facts come from maintained
    records and trusted sources, not model invention.
3.  **Transparent estimates:** show what is included, assumptions,
    source and last-verified date.
4.  **Meaningful variety:** recommendations should differ in experience,
    not merely name.
5.  **Guest-first:** users can discover and spin without creating an
    account.
6.  **No false certainty:** distinguish verified facts, estimates,
    seasonal information and unknown data.
7.  **Mobile-first:** the primary journey must work comfortably on a
    phone.

## 7. MVP user journey

1.  Visitor opens homepage.
2.  Selects **Start Your Trip**.
3.  Enters travel preferences.
4.  Reviews and submits preferences.
5.  System validates inputs and searches the destination catalogue.
6.  System filters destinations using hard constraints.
7.  System scores and diversifies eligible results.
8.  System returns exactly three recommendations, or explains why it
    cannot.
9.  User reviews the three cards and spins.
10. Server securely selects one of the three.
11. Frontend animates to the server-selected result.
12. User opens destination details.
13. User reviews estimates and itinerary.
14. User saves locally or creates a shareable trip link.

## 8. Functional requirements

### FR-01 --- Homepage

-   Explain the product in one clear headline.
-   Provide a primary "Start Your Trip" action.
-   Show curated destination discovery entry points.
-   Provide navigation to Explore, About, Contact, Privacy and Terms.
-   Avoid requiring login to begin.

### FR-02 --- Preference wizard

Required fields: - starting location (selected from supported
locations); - travel date or flexible date; - duration: few hours, one
day, two days, or three to five days; - budget per person in INR; -
number of travellers; - travel group: solo, friends, couple or family; -
one or more interests: nature, beach, adventure, culture, relaxation,
wildlife, food or similar curated taxonomy.

Optional fields: - transport mode: bike, car or public transport; -
accommodation preference when an overnight trip is selected; -
accessibility or mobility needs, if supported by reliable destination
data.

Behaviour: - show progress and validation; - preserve entered values
when navigating backward; - remember the last starting location locally
with user consent or clear browser storage behaviour; - support flexible
date exploration; - clearly state that budget is per person; - never
silently change a user-entered value.

### FR-03 --- Recommendation generation

-   Return exactly three eligible destinations for a successful result.
-   Apply hard filters before scoring.
-   Store the input snapshot and recommendation set for reproducibility.
-   Explain each match using factual fields from the database.
-   Ensure the three results are meaningfully diverse.
-   If fewer than three destinations qualify, do not fabricate results.
    Explain the constraint conflict and offer specific adjustments such
    as increasing budget, extending duration or broadening interests.

### FR-04 --- Recommendation card

Each card should show: - destination name and district; - representative
image with attribution where required; - category or experience label; -
approximate road distance and travel time, if available; - estimated
per-person cost and what it includes; - concise match explanation; -
material warnings or missing-data indicators; - action to view
destination details.

### FR-05 --- Roulette

-   Show the same three destinations returned by the recommendation
    service.
-   Give each destination equal probability (one-third).
-   Selection is performed on the server using a cryptographically
    secure random method.
-   Frontend animation must land on the server-selected destination.
-   A spin result is immutable for that recommendation set.
-   Prevent duplicate concurrent spins using an idempotency key or
    equivalent server-side control.
-   A fresh recommendation set is required for another spin in MVP.
-   Do not use animation timing or client-side randomness to determine
    the winner.

### FR-06 --- Destination detail

Display: - overview, district and map location; - gallery and image
attribution; - attractions and suggested activities; - travel
information from the selected starting point; - budget breakdown and
assumptions; - suggested itinerary; - best-season guidance where
verified; - entry fees, permit requirements and access restrictions
where verified; - facilities and accessibility information where
known; - source and last-verified timestamps; - clear labels for
estimated, verified, seasonal or unknown information.

### FR-07 --- Itinerary

-   Generate a practical sequence from available attraction, duration
    and route data.
-   Include travel buffers and meal breaks.
-   Avoid scheduling attractions outside verified opening windows.
-   Do not invent opening hours, permit availability or travel
    durations.
-   Clearly label an itinerary as suggested, not a confirmed booking.
-   Allow the user to copy or share it.

### FR-08 --- Save and share

-   Allow guest users to save a trip locally.
-   Allow a user to create a shareable trip URL.
-   Shared view must expose only intended trip information.
-   Use unguessable share tokens; do not expose sequential database IDs.
-   Provide WhatsApp and native share actions when supported.
-   Provide a copy-link fallback.

### FR-09 --- Admin destination management

Authorized administrators can: - create, edit, archive and publish
destinations; - manage districts, categories, attractions and images; -
manage cost items and calculation assumptions; - maintain access and
restriction information; - record sources, verification status and
verification dates; - preview a destination before publishing; - view
basic recommendation and engagement analytics.

A destination must not be published until required fields and editorial
checks pass.

### FR-10 --- Analytics

Track privacy-conscious events: - homepage_view; - start_trip_clicked; -
preferences_completed; - recommendations_generated; -
recommendations_insufficient; - roulette_spin_started; -
roulette_spin_completed; - destination_viewed; - itinerary_generated; -
trip_saved; - trip_shared.

Do not include precise personal location or sensitive free-text data in
analytics events.

## 9. Non-functional requirements

### Performance

-   Optimize for mobile networks and low-to-mid-range devices.
-   Lazy-load below-the-fold images.
-   Use responsive image sizes and modern formats where possible.
-   Cache static assets and public destination content.
-   Set measurable Core Web Vitals targets before public launch.

### Availability and resilience

-   Show graceful errors when maps, weather or AI providers are
    unavailable.
-   Core destination browsing should not depend on an LLM.
-   Never silently replace missing external data with fabricated values.

### Security and privacy

-   Enable Row Level Security on every table; guests get a Supabase
    anonymous identity and can read only their own trips.
-   Keep service-role and provider secrets in server-side function
    secrets.
-   Validate all inputs in Edge Functions; clients cannot write trip,
    recommendation or spin records directly.
-   Rate-limit expensive endpoints.
-   Protect admin routes and operations.
-   Minimize personal data collection.
-   Provide privacy, retention and deletion information.

### Accessibility

-   Keyboard-accessible controls.
-   Visible focus states.
-   Sufficient contrast.
-   Semantic headings and labels.
-   Reduced-motion support for roulette animation.
-   Do not rely on colour alone to convey warnings.

### PWA

-   Valid manifest and service worker.
-   Installable on supported browsers.
-   Offline access to previously cached public destination pages where
    feasible.
-   Clear indication when content may be stale.
-   Never imply cached access, prices or weather are current.

## 10. MVP acceptance criteria

The MVP is acceptable for beta when: 1. A guest can complete the
preference wizard on mobile and desktop. 2. Invalid or incomplete inputs
receive useful validation. 3. The recommendation service returns three
eligible results or a clear insufficient-results response. 4. Every
result can be traced to destination data and applied rules. 5. A spin
can select only one of the three returned destinations. 6. The
server-selected result and wheel animation always agree. 7. A user can
view destination information, cost assumptions and source dates. 8. An
itinerary does not assert unverified access or opening information. 9. A
user can save locally and share a trip safely. 10. Admins can maintain
and verify destination records. 11. Security, regression and essential
accessibility tests pass. 12. Production monitoring and error reporting
are active.

## 11. Risks and mitigations

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Risk                                Mitigation
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Outdated access or permit           Source tracking, expiry/review
  information                         dates, visible uncertainty

  Inaccurate budget                   Itemized assumptions, ranges,
                                      verification dates

  Too few eligible destinations       Expand curated catalogue; explain
                                      constraint conflict

  AI hallucination                    LLM only receives grounded data;
                                      validate output

  Similar recommendations             Category/experience diversity rules

  Third-party service outage          Graceful degradation and cached
                                      public content

  Poor mobile performance             Image optimization, lazy loading,
                                      performance testing

  Untrusted user submissions          Moderation and admin approval
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Product decisions and open questions

Baseline decisions: - Kerala-first launch. - Responsive website and
PWA. - Three recommendations per successful set. - Equal one-third
roulette probability. - Budget entered per person. - Guest-first
experience. - Curated destination database. - Rule-based filtering and
ranking; AI is optional for grounded explanations and itinerary
assistance. - Group rooms and voting deferred to Phase 2.

Resolve before implementation: - Minimum and maximum budget presets. -
Exact supported duration definitions. - Whether the first release
supports public transport estimates or only bike/car. - Which routing
and weather providers to use. - Data sources and editorial owner for
destination verification. - Whether guest trip sharing stores data
server-side or uses a signed, expiring public snapshot. - Retention
period for anonymous trip records.
